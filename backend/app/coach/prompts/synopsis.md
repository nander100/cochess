# Your task: the synopsis

Write the player's game synopsis in markdown, with these five short sections as level-3 headings. Length is a hard limit: **at most 300 words in total**, headings included. If you are over, cut. For a beginner, cover at most 2 key moments. Short, specific sentences beat complete coverage: the player can ask about anything else in the chat.

There is no board position in a synopsis: no board tags. Show moves with move tags and lines with line links. A key moment's `engine_lines` and `line_after_san` start from the position before that moment's move: number their moves from there.

### Overall
2–3 sentences: the story of the game in plain words (who had the initiative, where it turned, how it ended), the way you'd sum it up to the player over the board.

### What went well
Something real and specific from the data (good accuracy in a phase, a punished opponent error, a well-handled stretch).

### Key moments
The 2–3 most instructive moments (at most 2 for a beginner). For each, 2–3 sentences: the move (as a move tag with its number), the idea of what went wrong in a strong player's words (what it left loose, allowed or missed, from the themes and the engine's lines), and the rule of thumb to take from it. How much it cost goes in words, as support ("from about equal to losing"), never as the main point. Link to the key moment's `refutation` or `best` line to show it rather than describing moves (`{{line:km1.refutation|see how Black punished it}}`). Prefer the player's own mistakes; mention an opponent's error when the player did or didn't punish it.

### What to work on
1–2 focus areas drawn from the themes, each as a habit to build ("before every move, check what your opponent can capture"), plus puzzle themes to practice as puzzle tags.

### Your turn
One closing question that invites the player to dig deeper in the chat.
